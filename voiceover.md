## Chapter 1 — Open

It's Founder Tip Tuesday. Eighty-four people told Jason Fried they were coming to his event. Thirty-eight showed up. If you've ever built a plan on a list of people who said yes, that gap is the whole problem.

## Chapter 2 — The change at the door

Fried runs 37signals. In August he ran the breakfast again in Chicago, with one change at the door. When you registered, he charged you a hundred dollars. When you walked in, somebody handed you a crisp hundred dollar bill back.

## Chapter 3 — It costs you money

His words. It costs you money not to be there.

## Chapter 4 — Free vs $100

The free event got eighty-four yeses and thirty-eight people in the room. The hundred dollar event got fifty-five payments and fifty people in the room.

## Chapter 5 — 90% against 45%

That's a ninety percent show-rate, against forty-five percent for the free one. He doubled it by making the yes cost something, and he gave every dollar back to the people who meant it. The five hundred that the no-shows left behind went to charity.

## Chapter 6 — You are running a list

Now, you're not running a breakfast. But you are running a list. So what has anybody on that list spent to be there?

## Chapter 7 — Free to say

On my own lists, usually nothing. Send us the protocol and we'll take a look. We'd love to be a beta site. Come demo for the department sometime, when you're next in town. Put us down as interested. All of those are free to say. And any one of them can end up on the investor slide labeled design partner.

## Chapter 8 — Charge them time

You can't charge a hospital a hundred dollars. So charge them time instead, and give it straight back when they show up. Ask for a date on the calendar with the person who owns the budget. Ask who else will be in the room. Get one page signed before you ship a unit. None of that costs a site more than fifteen minutes. But a site that won't spend the fifteen minutes is still just a conversation.

## Chapter 9 — The question

The question I keep asking about my own pipeline is which of these yeses ever cost anybody anything. The ones that didn't, I can't count. And I've built plans on them anyway.
