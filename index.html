    Founder Tip Tuesday

    84 people said yes. 38 showed up.

    Jason Fried published both numbers in August, along with what he did next.

    Jason Fried, 37signals, August 28, 2026

    He charged $100 at registration, and handed it back at the door.

    In August he ran his 37signals breakfast in Chicago and changed one thing. Registration cost $100. On the way in, a person at the door handed each guest a crisp $100 bill.

    "It costs you money not to be there."
    Jason Fried, 37signals
    Same breakfast, one change

    Fewer signups. More people.

    Free

    84 said they would come. 38 showed.

    $100, handed back at the door

    55 paid. 50 showed.

    The show-rate
    90% vs 45%
    The $500 the no-shows left behind went to charity.
    Your turn

    You are not running a breakfast. But you are running a list.

    The pilot sites and design partners you are counting on. What has anybody on that list paid to be there?

    Free to say

    Yeses that cost nobody anything

    • Send us the protocol and we'll take a look.
    • We'd love to be a beta site.
    • Come demo for the department sometime.
    • Put us down as interested.
    Where they end up

    Any one of them can land on the investor slide labeled design partner.

    The email cost the site nothing to send. You are the one who then builds a forecast on it, and hands an investor a date they will hold you to.

    The move this week

    Charge them time instead. Then hand it straight back.

    You can't charge a hospital $100 to hold a pilot slot. Time you can charge.

    1. 1
      A date with whoever owns the budget
      The person who signs, not the surgeon who loves it.
    2. 2
      The names of who else is in the room
      Nobody can answer that without asking around inside.
    3. 3
      One page signed before a unit ships
      Scope, dates, what counts as done.
    4. 4
      Give it all back the moment they show
      Fried's version of this was a $100 bill in the hand at the door.
    The threshold

    Fifteen minutes.

    None of those asks costs a site more than that. A site that won't spend them hasn't told you anything you can plan around, and a pipeline slide with a date on it is a plan.

    The question

    Which of your yeses ever cost anybody anything?

    The ones that didn't, you can't count. And plans get built on them anyway.
